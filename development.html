Personal Development

The lessons taught in The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse and The Five Laws of Gold are some of the best advice I have read about managing money. They reminded me of many of the financial lessons my father has taught me throughout my life, especially the importance of working hard, being wise with my finances, budgeting, saving, and planning for the future.

The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse explains several principles that can help a person improve their financial situation. The main ideas include saving part of your income, controlling spending, making your money work, protecting your wealth, improving your ability to earn, and making careful financial decisions.

One lesson that stood out to me was the idea that for every ten coins I put in, I should spend only nine. This means that I should save at least ten percent of what I earn before spending the rest. This principle shows that building wealth starts with developing the discipline to keep part of your income instead of spending everything.

I connected strongly with these lessons because I have already experienced the importance of saving and budgeting in my own life.

The Five Laws of Gold

The Five Laws of Gold focuses on how money can be protected and grown once it has been saved. The lessons emphasize saving consistently, making money work through wise investments, protecting investments, understanding where money is being placed, and seeking knowledgeable advice.

These principles taught me that earning money is only the beginning. I also need to understand how to manage, protect, and grow the money I earn. I should not invest simply because something promises quick profits. Instead, I should understand the investment and make informed decisions.

How These Lessons Apply to My Life

The lessons from both chapters remind me of the financial advice my father has given me from a young age. He always taught me to work hard, budget my money, control unnecessary spending, and think about the future instead of only focusing on what I want in the present.

I remember when I was in seventh grade and my father gave me weekly lunch money. Instead of spending all of it, I started taking out $100 each day and putting it aside. I continued doing this for about two months. At first, I saved the money because I wanted to buy things I liked, such as earphones, snacks, and items from the game shop.

Although I was saving, I eventually realized that I could put the money toward something more important. Later, I needed a new phone, and my father told me that I would have to raise half of the money while he would pay the other half. This changed the way I looked at saving. I stopped spending as much on things I wanted and focused on saving for something I actually needed.

This experience connects directly to the lesson in The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse. It showed me that saving money is not enough by itself. I also need to have a purpose for my savings and make decisions that will benefit me in the future.

I also want to apply the investment principles from The Five Laws of Gold more strongly in my life. After returning from my Work and Travel experience, I plan to invest a portion of the money I earned in stocks and allow the investment to grow over a long period of time. My goal is to give the money around ten to fifteen years to grow while I continue learning about investing and making informed decisions.

Instead of looking for quick profits, I want to focus on building wealth gradually. I want to reach a point where my money can continue working for me and provide financial security later in life.

My Biggest Takeaway

My biggest takeaway from these lessons is that financial success requires discipline and good decision-making. Earning money is only the first step. I also have to know how to save it, manage it, protect it, and make it grow.

These lessons have encouraged me to think more seriously about my financial future. I want to develop good financial habits now so that I can benefit from them later. The goal is not simply to have money, but to use it wisely and build a secure future for myself and my family.
