LLM's: A Tool, Not a Crutch

Artificial intelligence tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot have become valuable resources for students. However, I believe that these tools should be used to develop our skills rather than replace our ability to think. AI should be viewed as another tool in our utility belt that can help us advance our studies and prepare for the future.

LLMs can improve learning when they are used to explain difficult concepts, providing examples, helping students identify mistakes, and encourage further research. Research has found that ChatGPT can have positive effects on academic performance, motivation, and higher-order thinking when it is used appropriately as part of the learning process (Deng et al., 2025). Similarly, research involving university students found that ChatGPT can be useful for developing complex thinking skills (Romero-Rodríguez et al., 2023).

for exmaple, a student learning programming can use AI to understand why a piece of code is not working, but should then study the explanation and try to solve similar problems independently. This approach allows AI to support learning instead of doing the learning for the students.

The greatest danger is becoming dependent on AI for quick answers. Simply copying an AI-generated response may allow a student to complete an assignment, but it does not guarantee that the student understands the material. Overreliance can weaken important skills such as reasoning, problem-solving, creativity, and independent research. AI can also provide incorrect or misleading information, so students must question, verify, and critically evaluate what it produces.

In my view, AI should broaden our horizons rather than become a one-stop shop for answers. Students should use it to ask better questions, explore different ideas, understand difficult topics, and discover information they may not have considered. We should also challenge what AI tells us instead of automatically accepting every response.

Ultimately, AI is most useful when the student remains in control of the learning process. Used responsibly, LLMs can help students become better learners, problem-solvers, and researchers. They should be treated as a tool that strengthens our abilities, not a crutch that replaces them.
